Title: The Voicemail

Paul came home one evening to find seventeen missed calls on his phone. All from the same number. A number he did not recognize.

And one voicemail.

He almost deleted it. He got spam calls constantly. But something made him press play.

The voice on the voicemail was his own.

Not a similar voice. Not someone doing an impression. His voice. Exactly. Every inflection. Every small habit of speech that he had never noticed in himself but recognized instantly now with a cold sick certainty.

The message said this.

Paul. Listen to me carefully. Do not go upstairs tonight. Whatever you hear up there, whatever it sounds like, do not go up. Lock your bedroom door. Do not open it until morning. I know this sounds insane. I know you do not believe me. But you have to listen. I am begging you. Please.

Then the message ended.

Paul stood in his hallway for a long time.

He was alone in the house. He lived alone. He had lived alone for two years.

He told himself it was a prank. A very good prank by someone who knew him well. He went to the kitchen. Made dinner. Ate at the table. Tried not to think about the voicemail.

At nine o clock he heard something upstairs.

Footsteps. Slow. Heavy. Moving from one end of the upper hall to the other and back again.

Paul gripped the edge of the table.

The footsteps stopped outside the door at the top of the stairs.

Silence.

Then a knock. One knock. Patient. Deliberate.

Paul remembered the voicemail.

He went to his bedroom on the ground floor. Locked the door. Lay on the bed fully dressed with the light on.

All night he heard the footsteps above him. All night he heard the slow patient knock every hour.

In the morning the sounds stopped. He unlocked the door. Stood in the hallway.

His phone was buzzing.

A new voicemail. Same number.

He pressed play.

His own voice again. Tired now. Relieved.

You made it. Good. Now listen. Tonight is going to be harder. Tonight it is going to sound like someone you love.

Paul stared at his phone.

From upstairs came the sound of footsteps again.

And then a voice he recognized.

His mother's voice.

Paul. Come up. It is me. Come and open the door.

Paul locked himself in his bedroom and did not move.

He is still waiting for a voicemail to tell him it is over.

It has not come yet.
