She had collected antiques for thirty years. She had a sense for objects with histories. She could walk through a market and feel which pieces had something in them.

She usually avoided those pieces.

The box was different. It called to her in a way she had never experienced. Not felt. Called. An almost audible suggestion that she should take it home.

She bought it. Against her better judgment. Against thirty years of instinct.

The box was locked. She could not find the mechanism. She could not find a keyhole. It was simply sealed.

She kept it on her shelf for two weeks. She found herself talking to it. Explaining her day. Asking it questions.

She had never done anything like that with any other object.

On the fifteenth night she woke to find the box open on her bedside table. She did not know how she had opened it. She did not know how she had gotten out of bed.

Inside the box was nothing. Empty.

And yet she understood, without knowing how she understood it, that what had been in the box was now in her. And had been since the moment she bought it.

And that the box was just the delivery method.
