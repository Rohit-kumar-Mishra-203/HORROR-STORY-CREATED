The book was found in the wall of a house being demolished. Old. Handwritten. In a language that turned out to be an archaic dialect no longer spoken.

The translator who worked on it said it was a journal. Personal. Mundane for the most part. Someone's daily life in a time she could not precisely determine.

Except for the last thirty pages.

The last thirty pages were instructions. Detailed. Specific. She translated them carefully.

They described how to put something inside a book. Not physically. Something else. A presence. A watcher. The instructions said the watcher would remain dormant until someone read the instructions aloud.

She had translated the instructions aloud. Working through them carefully to understand the dialect.

That night she felt it. Something in the book's vicinity that had not been there before. An attention. As if she were now being watched from a distance of about two feet. Consistently.

She put the book in a sealed container. She submitted her translation with a note recommending the original be destroyed.

It was not destroyed. It is in a university archive.

She still feels watched. Two feet to her left. Wherever she goes.
