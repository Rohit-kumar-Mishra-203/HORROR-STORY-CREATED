Title - The Locket That Could Not Be Opened
Margaret Holt received the locket in , mailed in a small brown package from a return address that turned out to be a vacant lot in the east end of the city. No sender's name. No accompanying note. The package had been posted through normal channels, stamped and processed, received by the postal service in the ordinary way, and delivered to her doorstep on a Thursday morning in February. Inside the package was a locket of tarnished silver, oval, approximately the diameter of a large coin, with a small latch mechanism on one side and a loop for a chain at the top. It appeared old. The silver had the particular dullness of an object that has not been polished in decades. The latch was present and visible and apparently functional.
It would not open. Margaret tried it for twenty minutes. Her husband tried it. A jeweler examined it with proper tools and said the latch was sound, that there was no mechanical reason the locket should not open, that he could see the latch moving partially when pressure was applied but that it would not complete its travel. He said this with the expression of a man who understands that what he is describing is not mechanically possible and who has decided, professionally, not to pursue that understanding. He returned the locket without charging her. She wore it anyway, on a chain she provided herself, because it felt and she wrote this in a letter to her cousin, in the careful language of a woman who understood the social costs of saying strange things plainly it felt wrong to put it away. As though putting it in a drawer would constitute a decision she was not entitled to make.
The dreams began in the third week. A woman inside the locket. She knew this in the dream the way you know things in dreams, completely and without evidence. A woman in a space that was larger on the inside than a locket should allow a room, perhaps, or something room-shaped, lit by no light she could identify, furnished with nothing she could name, but navigable, livable, a space in which a person could exist over a long period of time if they were the kind of person who could adapt. The woman in the dream had hands that Margaret could see clearly. They were pressed against the inside of the silver. They were larger than the locket. Impossibly larger. As though the interiority of the object did not correspond to its exterior dimensions. The hands moved occasionally, shifting their pressure, exploring the surface, finding the seam where the latch was, pressing.
Margaret wore the locket for twenty-nine years. She did not speak of the dreams after the first few months, having found that speaking of them produced a concern in others that she did not want and that she did not feel was warranted. She was not frightened of the dreams. She was not frightened of the locket. She was curious about it, in the way that one can be curious about a thing that is clearly beyond one's capacity to explain, and she wore it every day and went about her life and in the dreams the woman inside continued to press her too-large hands against the silver interior and the room that should not have existed continued to exist.
Margaret died in at the age of seventy-three. The locket was on her person at the funeral home. When the director's assistant went to remove it to prepare the body, the latch opened. He looked inside. He reported this to the director as a minor curiosity worth noting. He said the inside was larger than it should have been. That when he looked into the open locket, the interior surface appeared to recede further than the silver casing allowed, as though the space inside was deeper than the object that contained it. He said this in the same tone he might use to describe a slight printing error on a form notable, mildly puzzling, not something that required extended thought.
The locket was buried with Margaret, at her daughter's instruction. Her daughter did not explain the instruction. When the daughter died in , her own instruction in her will stated only that the grave was not to be disturbed. The grave has not been disturbed. No one who has investigated this story has seen the locket since . No one, as far as can be established, has looked inside it since the funeral director's assistant closed it in the preparation room and went to attend to other things, and whether the locket remained closed after he shut it, whether the latch held, whether whatever was inside remained inside, cannot be confirmed.
