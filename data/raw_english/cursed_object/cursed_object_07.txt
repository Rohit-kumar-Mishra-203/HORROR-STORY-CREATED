The coin was old. Very old. The metal was wrong in a way she could not identify. The markings were not any currency she recognized.

She kept it on her desk. She liked the way it felt. Heavier than it should have been for its size. Warm in a way metal should not be warm.

Decisions came more easily after she started keeping it. She would flip it when she was uncertain. Heads she went left. Tails she went right. The coin always seemed to land on the side that led somewhere interesting.

After six months she realized she was making all her decisions this way. She could not remember the last time she had decided something on her own.

She tried to stop using the coin.

She put it in a drawer.

She found herself going to the drawer twenty times a day. A pull she could not resist. A need she could not explain.

She tried to get rid of it. She could not. Her hands would not let her put it down long enough to leave it somewhere.

She still has it. She still uses it for every decision. And she has stopped wondering why she finds it so difficult to decide anything without it.

The coin is always warm. It is always heavier than it should be. And sometimes when it lands she is certain, for just a moment, that it did not fall randomly.
