The letters had been in the attic for forty years. Tied with ribbon. Written in a hand he recognized from other family documents.

His great aunt. Dead before he was born.

He read the letters. Personal correspondence. A love story over several years. Then a gap. Then letters in a different character. Darker. Confused.

The last letter described something she had bought. An object. She did not describe it precisely. She wrote about what it did instead.

She wrote I can hear it thinking. She wrote it shows me things I should not be able to see. She wrote I am afraid of what I will do if I keep it but I am more afraid of what will happen if I give it away because I do not think I can bear for it to belong to someone else.

There were no more letters after that.

He found the object at the bottom of the box. A small carved figure. No identifiable material. No identifiable origin.

He picked it up.

It was warm.

And immediately he understood what she had meant. About hearing it think. About not being able to bear the idea of it belonging to someone else.

He put it back in the box. He closed the box.

He has not opened it again.

But he thinks about it every day. It is the first thing he thinks about in the morning. He suspects it will be the last thing he thinks about at the end.
