The night shift at the hospital was quiet. It was always quiet on the fourth floor. That was where they kept the patients who were not expected to make it through the night.

Nurse Carla had worked that floor for six years. She had seen things she did not talk about. The monitors going flat and then resuming for no medical reason. The way some patients seemed to be having conversations with empty chairs. The cold that gathered in room four twelve at three in the morning without fail.

What she never talked about was the man in the grey coat who walked the corridor every night at two fifteen. Tall. Unhurried. Stopping at each room and looking through the window.

She had asked a colleague once. The colleague had looked at her carefully and said what man.

Carla had stopped asking.

But she noticed that on the nights the man stopped longest at a particular room the patient in that room was gone by morning. She had started to think of him as a schedule. A reminder of what the fourth floor was really for.
