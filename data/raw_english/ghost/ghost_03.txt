The old man next door died on a Tuesday. By Wednesday his lights were on again.

Owen noticed from his kitchen window. He told himself the family must have come to collect things. But the car in the driveway was not one he recognized. And the shape moving past the windows was too tall for old Mr. Harlow and too thin for anyone he had seen entering the house.

He knocked the next morning. Nobody answered. The lights were off.

That night they were on again. And the shape was there. Walking slowly from room to room.

Owen called the police. They checked the house. Empty. No sign of entry. The lights were not on when they arrived.

That Friday Owen was woken at three in the morning by a knock at his front door. He looked through the peephole. Old Mr. Harlow was standing on his porch. In the same clothes he had been buried in.

He said I think I went to the wrong house.
