Title: Room 9

The motel was the only one for fifty miles. Jason had no choice. His car had broken down on a highway that cut through nothing but flat dark farmland and a sky full of cold stars.

The woman at the front desk was old. She handed him a key without looking up.

Room 9, she said. End of the row.

Jason asked if there were other guests.

She shook her head.

He walked to room 9. The other rooms all had dark windows. All were silent. He unlocked the door and went inside.

The room was ordinary enough. A bed. A television. A bathroom. A painting on the wall of a field at dusk.

He lay down without undressing. He was asleep in minutes.

He woke at three in the morning.

The television was on. Static. White noise filling the dark room.

He reached for the remote and stopped.

There was someone sitting in the chair in the corner. A man. Completely still. Watching the static on the television.

Jason could not move. Could not speak. He lay frozen in the bed watching the man in the chair.

After what felt like hours the man slowly turned his head. He looked at Jason. His eyes were completely white.

You are in my room, the man said.

Jason grabbed his keys and ran. He did not stop at the front desk. He ran to the road and kept running until the lights of a passing truck found him and stopped.

The driver took him to the next town. Jason told him what happened.

The driver was quiet for a moment. Then he said, that motel has been closed for fifteen years. Nobody has worked there. Nobody has stayed there.

Jason said that was impossible. He had spoken to the woman at the desk. He had a key in his hand.

He opened his hand.

There was no key. There was nothing.

But on his palm, as if pressed there by a finger, was a single red mark.

The number 9.
