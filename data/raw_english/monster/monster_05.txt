The mining company hit the cavern on the forty second day of drilling. It should not have been there. The geological surveys had shown nothing.

The cavern was enormous. Their instruments could not determine the far wall.

They sent in a remote camera. The footage showed a space larger than any known natural cavern. Perfectly smooth walls. As if shaped by something other than geological processes.

And in the center of the cavern floor something large was curled. Motionless. Apparently asleep.

The company geologist said stop. Everyone stop.

The drill operator asked why.

The geologist did not answer. He was watching the footage from the remote camera.

The thing in the cavern had moved. Slightly. The way something moves when a sound disturbs its sleep.

The geologist said seal the borehole. Do it now.

The borehole was sealed.

The company filed their report indicating the site was geologically unsuitable for mining.

The geologist never worked in mining again. When asked why he said he had decided to pursue work in a field where disturbing things that have been sleeping for a very long time was not a professional risk.
