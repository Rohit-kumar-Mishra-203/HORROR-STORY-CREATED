Title: The Shadow That Stayed

Everyone has a shadow. Sarah had two.

She noticed it first on a Tuesday morning in October. She was walking to work in the early light and glanced down at the pavement and saw them. Her shadow stretching out ahead of her in the low autumn sun. And beside it, slightly behind, a second shadow. Same height. Same shape. Moving with her.

She stopped. The second shadow stopped.

She looked around. There was nobody near her. Nobody who could cast that second shadow. The street was empty.

She walked on quickly, telling herself it was a trick of the light. A reflection from a window. Something ordinary with an ordinary explanation.

But the second shadow was there again that evening. On the wall of her apartment as she moved through the rooms. Her shadow and something else's shadow. Following her from room to room.

She called her friend Tom. He came over and saw nothing unusual. Just one shadow. Her shadow.

When he left she stood in front of the bright lamp in her living room and looked at the wall.

Two shadows.

She reached out and touched the second shadow with her hand.

The second shadow grabbed her hand.

She screamed and pulled away. Her hand was ice cold where the shadow had touched it.

That night she could not sleep. She lay in the dark and watched the ceiling. She did not want to turn the light on. She did not want to see two shadows on the wall.

At three in the morning she heard breathing. Close. Right next to her ear. Slow and cold and deliberate.

She turned her head.

On the pillow beside her was an indentation. As if a head were resting there. As if something were lying right beside her in the bed.

She ran from the apartment and never went back.

But it did not matter.

Wherever she goes now, in any light, against any wall or floor or ceiling, there are always two shadows.

And sometimes, when she is very still and the room is very quiet, she can feel cold breath on the back of her neck.

And she knows that whatever it is, it is never going to leave.
