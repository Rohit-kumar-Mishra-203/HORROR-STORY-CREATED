The photographer had been shooting abandoned buildings for years. She had a good eye for emptiness.

After the session in the old textile mill she drove home and downloaded her photos and made dinner and went to bed.

In the morning she was fine. Normal.

Except for the mirror.

She caught herself in the bathroom mirror and stopped. Her reflection was looking at her with an expression she had not felt herself making. Patient. Watchful. Like someone who has just arrived somewhere after a very long journey and is quietly taking stock of the new surroundings.

She raised her hand. Her reflection raised its hand.

But for a fraction of a second the expression did not change.

She spent the day watching herself in windows and reflective surfaces. The expression was usually right. But sometimes. In the gap between moments. Something else looked back.

She went back to the textile mill. She stood in the middle of the main floor. She said out loud whoever came back with me I need you to go.

She felt something shift. Like a change in air pressure.

The drive home was fine. The mirror was fine.

She still checks. Every morning. Just in case.
