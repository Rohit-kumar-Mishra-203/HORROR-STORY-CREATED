The old priest had seen many things in forty years of ministry. He had conducted three exorcisms. He considered them all failures.

Not because the things had not left. They had all left. But in each case something of them remained. A stain. A trace.

He knew this from the way the people were afterward. Mostly themselves. But with a shadow. Something that was not quite them looking out from behind their eyes occasionally.

He had come to believe that complete separation was not always possible. That sometimes a presence leaves a mark just by being there long enough.

He had also come to believe that not all of what he had been taught about possession was correct.

Some of what came in was looking for refuge rather than a host. Some of it was old and frightened rather than malevolent. Some of it had been wandering for so long it had forgotten why it started or what it was looking for.

He still removed them. That was his work.

But in his private journals he wrote that he had begun to feel that what he was doing was less like battle and more like eviction. And that he was not always sure who had the better claim to be there.
