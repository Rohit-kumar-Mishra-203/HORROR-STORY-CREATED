Story : The Frost-Maiden of the Cairngorms
To the inhabitants of the Scottish Highlands, winter was a respected, formidable adversary. It was a season of roaring hearths, wool blankets, and stories told while the wind howled like a wounded beast against the thick stone walls of the crofts. But to Eira, winter was not a season; it was an extension of her own pulse.

Eira was the Frost-Maiden. She resided at the absolute summit of Ben Macdui, the second highest peak in the Cairngorms, living in a cavern sculpted from glacial ice that refracted the starlight into a thousand shades of blue. Her hair was the color of fresh snowfall, her eyes the sharp, clear azure of a frozen loch, and her cloak was woven from shed ptarmigan feathers and strands of pure, crystallized rime.

Her magic was the magic of the deep freeze. She painted the frost on the heather, guided the hibernating creatures into their deep sleep, and ensured the glaciers held firm until the spring melt. She was a creature of balance, a necessary, beautiful harshness.

But as July arrived on the calendar, the Cairngorms were not blooming with purple heather and running with meltwater. Instead, they were suffocating beneath an eternal, deadly blizzard.

This was no natural storm. The snow falling from the bruised, iron-grey sky was not the pure, silent white of Eira s domain. It was tainted with a sickly, rust-colored hue, and it fell with unnatural, horizontal violence. The wind did not whistle; it screamed in agony. The burns and rivers were frozen solid, not with clear ice, but with a dense, black permafrost that killed the fish and poisoned the roots of the ancient Caledonian pines.

Eira was dying. Her magic was tethered to the natural cycle of the earth, and this artificial, necrotic winter was parasitic. It was feeding on the mountain s life force, and by extension, hers.

She sat cross-legged on the floor of her ice cavern, her breathing shallow, her skin taking on a dangerous, translucent pallor. She placed her bare hands flat against the glacial floor, pushing past the agonizing, biting cold of the corrupted ice, diving her consciousness deep into the bedrock of Ben Macdui.

She listened to the stone. The mountain remembered everything.

Through the static of the howling wind, she found the source. It was an ancient, bitter memory. Three hundred years ago, a Highland chieftain named Alastair MacFie had been betrayed by his own kin on the slopes of this very mountain. As he bled out in the snow, he had cursed the land with his dying breath, binding his agonizing death to a ruby-hilted dirk stolen from his belt by the traitors. He swore that until the blade was returned to the mountain's heart, the Cairngorms would know the exact, freezing despair of his final moments. The sun would never warm the rocks again.

For centuries, the dirk had been lost, the curse lying dormant. But a week ago, the resonance had shifted. Someone had brought the blade back to the Highlands.

Eira knew she could not wait for the storm to break. It never would. She had to find the dirk and break the blood-curse, or the Cairngorms would become a dead, frozen wasteland forever.

She stood up, pulling her feather cloak tightly around her shoulders. She did not walk out into the storm; she dissolved into it.

Eira rode the violent wind, a phantom of frost and pale light navigating the blinding, rust-colored blizzard. The cold was profound, a biting, malicious entity that tried to rip the magic from her veins, but she was the Frost-Maiden. She drew the ambient, natural chill of the upper atmosphere into herself, using it as a shield against the dark magic.

She tracked the heavy, metallic stench of the blood-curse down the mountain, leaving the high peaks and descending into the usually lush valley of Glenmore.

The valley was a graveyard. Flocks of sheep stood frozen solid in the fields like macabre statues. The ancient pines had splintered and cracked under the weight of the black ice. At the center of the valley, beside the frozen expanse of Loch Morlich, sat a sprawling, modernized hunting lodge.

The lodge was heavily fortified. The windows were shuttered with iron, and thick, black smoke belched from its three massive chimneys, desperately fighting the encroaching cold. But the rust-colored snow was piling high against the stone walls, slowly burying the structure alive.

Eira materialized on the front porch. The heavy oak door was barred from the inside, reinforced with steel. Eira didn't knock. She pressed her palm flat against the wood.

She didn't use heat to break it; she used absolute zero. She dropped the temperature of the moisture within the oak's cellular structure so rapidly that the water expanded with explosive force. With a sound like a cannon shot, the heavy door splintered into a thousand jagged pieces of frozen shrapnel.

Eira stepped into the lodge, surrounded by a swirling halo of white mist.

The grand living room was a scene of desperate survival. Expensive antique furniture had been smashed to pieces and thrown into a roaring, massive fireplace. Huddled near the flames, wrapped in a half-dozen heavy wool blankets, was a man.

He was in his late forties, wealthy, and terrified. His name was Lachlan MacFie a distant, thoroughly modern descendant of the very men who had betrayed the ancient chieftain. On the heavy oak coffee table beside him, resting on a velvet cloth, was the source of the apocalypse: a magnificent, ancient dirk, its silver blade etched with runes, its hilt adorned with a massive, blood-red ruby.

"Who... what are you?" Lachlan stammered, pulling a hunting rifle from beneath his blankets and pointing it at her with shaking hands. "Stay back! I'll shoot!"

"You are freezing to death in a room with a bonfire," Eira said, her voice sounding like the cracking of glacial ice. "Mundane fire cannot warm a supernatural chill. You have brought a blood-curse to my mountain, Lachlan MacFie."

"It's just a storm!" Lachlan yelled, his teeth chattering violently. "A freak anomaly! I bought that dirk at auction in London for fifty thousand pounds. It s family heritage! I came here to celebrate, not to freeze in the dark!"

"It is a tether," Eira corrected, stepping forward. The temperature in the room plummeted with her advance. The flames in the hearth shrank, turning from bright orange to a sickly, struggling blue. "Your ancestors spilled blood for that gold. Now, the mountain is claiming its due. Give me the blade, so I may return it to the stone and break the curse."

"No!" Lachlan scrambled forward, abandoning his rifle to snatch the dirk from the table, clutching it to his chest. "It's mine! It's worth a fortune!"

The moment his flesh touched the cursed metal, the storm outside reacted.

A deafening, howling shriek shook the foundations of the lodge. The boarded-up windows violently imploded. The rust-colored snow blasted into the room, carried by a wind so forceful it extinguished the massive fire in an instant, plunging the room into gloom.

The blood-magic surged, realizing it was being threatened by the Frost-Maiden. It didn't just swirl in the air; it coalesced. The rust-colored snow and the black ice from the loch outside rushed into the room, forming a towering, horrific elemental figure. It was a beast of pure, jagged frost, its eyes glowing with the same malevolent red light as the ruby in the dirk.

It was the manifestation of the chieftain s dying hatred.

The ice-beast roared, a sound of grinding glaciers, and lunged not at Lachlan, but at Eira.

Eira stood her ground. The beast swung a massive arm of jagged black ice, aiming to decapitate her. Eira raised her hand.

"Stillness," she commanded.

She didn't try to melt the beast; she imposed the absolute, unyielding order of deep winter upon the chaotic, violent storm magic. A wall of pure, crystalline hexagonal frost erupted from the floorboards, intercepting the beast's strike. The black ice shattered against the perfect geometric strength of the natural frost.

The beast shrieked, recoiling. It opened its maw, spewing a torrent of parasitic, rust-colored snow meant to drain Eira s life force.

Eira countered by weaving the ambient moisture in the room into a swirling vortex of protective rime. The necrotic snow hit her vortex and was instantly purified, stripped of its blood-magic by the sheer, overwhelming cold of Eira s domain.

"You are a ghost of anger," Eira said, her eyes blazing with azure light as she advanced on the towering elemental. "But I am the winter itself. You cannot freeze the frost."

She thrust both hands forward. She bypassed the physical form of the ice-beast and targeted the magical tether anchoring it to the mortal plane: the ruby dirk still clutched in the terrified, catatonic Lachlan's hands.

Eira pushed the temperature of the ruby down past the point of simple freezing, down toward the theoretical limit of thermodynamics. The magical energy trapped within the gemstone violently resisted, glowing with blinding, searing red heat.

The clash of absolute cold and cursed heat created a shockwave that threw Lachlan across the room. The dirk clattered to the floorboards.

Eira focused all her ancient, mountainous will onto the blade.

The ruby, unable to withstand the profound, rapid shift in molecular tension, exploded.

It didn't shatter into shards; it detonated into a cloud of fine, red dust. The silver blade of the dirk cracked down the middle with a sharp, ringing snap.

Instantly, the massive ice-elemental froze mid-strike. The dark, necrotic magic animating it evaporated, its tether severed. The beast collapsed into a harmless pile of ordinary, mundane slush on the Persian rug.

Outside, the screaming wind abruptly stopped. The deafening roar of the blizzard was replaced by an absolute, stunned silence.

Eira walked over to the broken remains of the dirk. She knelt, scooped the red dust and the shattered silver into her pale hands, and stood up. She looked at Lachlan, who was cowering in the corner, staring at her in shock.

"The debt is paid," she said softly. "The mountain has its blood back. Do not bring your greed to the Cairngorms again."

Eira turned and walked out through the shattered doorway.

The sky above was already clearing. The heavy, iron-grey clouds were breaking apart, revealing the brilliant, warm gold of the July sun. The rust-colored snow was already beginning to melt, turning into clear, rushing water that would soon revive the poisoned burns.

Eira closed her eyes, feeling the warmth of the sun on her face a sensation she usually avoided, but today, it felt like a profound victory. She let out a long, foggy breath, dissolving her physical form into a gentle, cool breeze, and began the long journey back up to the high peaks, leaving the summer to reclaim its rightful place in the valleys below.
