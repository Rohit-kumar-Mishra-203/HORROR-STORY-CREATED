Story : The Mirror-Witch of the Manor
Highcross Manor did not display paintings, tapestries, or mounted hunting trophies. The ancestral home of Lord Alaric Vance was decorated exclusively with mirrors. They lined the vast, drafty corridors, flanked the grand sweeping staircases, and dominated every parlor and bedchamber. There were massive, gilded pier glasses that reached the vaulted ceilings, oval looking-glasses framed in tarnished silver, and convex mirrors that distorted the gothic architecture into unsettling, bulbous curves.

To the outside world, Lord Vance was an eccentric aristocrat with a vanity bordering on the obsessive. He had not visibly aged a day in forty years, maintaining the sharp, pale features and raven hair of a man in his prime.

To Anya, a parlor maid whose entire existence was defined by a bucket of vinegar-water and a chamois cloth, the mirrors were simply a nightmare of endless labor.

Anya was nineteen, invisible to the aristocratic guests, and fiercely observant. She knew the manor intimately. She knew which floorboards creaked, which drafts meant a storm was coming, and she knew that the mirrors in Highcross Manor were profoundly, unnervingly cold. It wasn't the natural chill of glass in a drafty house; it was an active, draining cold that leached the warmth from her fingers through the cleaning rag.

Her life changed on the night of the Winter Solstice.

Anya had been tasked with polishing the Grand Hall looking-glass a monstrosity of Venetian crystal and blackened bronze after a lavish midnight banquet. The hall was empty, lit only by the dying embers in the massive hearth. Exhausted, Anya leaned her forehead against the glass for a brief moment, letting out a heavy, shuddering sigh.

Her breath plumed on the cold surface, leaving a patch of grey condensation.

As Anya raised her cloth to wipe it away, the condensation began to move. It wasn't dripping; it was swirling with deliberate intent. The fog on the glass rapidly condensed into a single, sharp word written from the inside out:

Anya froze, her heart hammering against her ribs. She stared at the glass. Slowly, agonizingly, a face pressed forward from the dark depths of the mirror. It was not a reflection. It was a woman, her features drawn and translucent, her eyes wide with a centuries-old terror. She was dressed in the high-collared lace of the previous century.

Anya didn't scream. A life of servitude had taught her that making noise only brought punishment. Instead, she leaned closer, her breath catching in her throat. She raised a trembling finger and traced a question mark in the fog on the outside of the glass.

The woman s ghostly hand mirrored Anya s from the inside. The fog shifted again, forming new words: ALARIC. SOULS FOR YOUTH. SILVER TRAP.

The horrifying truth clicked into place. Lord Vance wasn't vain; he was a warlock. He wasn't preserving his reflection; he was preserving his life by consuming the lifeforce of others, trapping their souls in the silver backing of his precious mirrors to fuel his unnatural youth. Highcross Manor wasn't an estate; it was a sprawling, glass-paneled prison.

How? Anya traced on the glass. How do I free you?

The woman hesitated, her eyes darting nervously as if expecting Alaric to appear from the shadows. Then, she wrote: SYMMETRY. LIGHT. THE SILVER REMEMBERS.

That night, back in her cramped, windowless quarters in the servants' wing, Anya did not sleep. She pulled a tiny, cracked hand mirror from her washstand. If Alaric used magic to trap them, magic must be able to release them. The ghost had mentioned symmetry and light.

Anya was not a born witch like the women in the old fairy tales. She had no wand, no ancient bloodline. But she had something Alaric did not: she intimately understood the physical nature of the glass. She knew how light bent through the beveled edges, how silver nitrate adhered to the silica, and how a reflection was merely a bouncing of energy.

She began to experiment. She discovered that reflective magic was a science of precise angles and reversed intentions. To cast a spell into a mirror, one had to think backward, moving the left hand to affect the right, speaking in perfect, palindromic cadence.

She practiced for weeks. She learned to catch the light of a single candle in her cracked hand mirror and bounce it perfectly into the shadows, manipulating the reflection to move physical objects a dropped hairpin, a heavy iron key. She learned to mix her vinegar and polishing chalk with coarse sea salt, creating a compound that didn't just clean the glass, but scrubbed away the residual dark magic clinging to the frames.

When she was ready, she began the jailbreak.

She started small. In the dusty, unused guest rooms of the west wing, Anya would wait until the precise moment the moonlight hit the windowpanes. She would position herself before a mirror, holding her chamois cloth soaked in her salted mixture.

She would trace symmetrical, geometric wards on the glass while whispering incantations in perfect reverse. "Erutcurts eht kaerb, revlis eht dneb." (Bend the silver, break the structure).

She would angle her small hand mirror to catch the moonlight, bouncing a concentrated beam of pure, lunar energy directly into the center of the ward.

The glass would hiss. The thick, dark silver backing would momentarily dissolve into liquid light, and with a soft, ethereal sigh, a trapped soul would slip through the fracture, dissolving into the night air to finally find peace. The mirror would then shatter a quiet, controlled spiderweb of cracks that left the glass opaque and entirely dead.

Over three months, Anya quietly emptied the west wing. She freed a stable boy taken in the s, a rival politician from the s, and a dozen others who had simply asked too many questions about the Lord of Highcross. To cover her tracks, she spread rumors among the staff of a foundation settling, causing the glass to crack under the pressure of the shifting stone.

But Alaric Vance was old, and he was cunning.

He began to notice the subtle shift in the ambient magic of his home. The house felt lighter, less oppressive. He noticed the cracked mirrors in the west wing, and while the foundation story was plausible, warlocks do not survive for centuries by ignoring coincidences.

The climax arrived on the night of a violent spring thunderstorm.

Anya had grown bold. She decided it was time to free the souls in the Grand Hall, including the lace-collared woman who had first spoken to her. She crept into the hall at two in the morning, the sound of her footsteps masked by the torrential rain and booming thunder.

She approached the massive Venetian mirror. She applied her salted chalk, drew the reversing wards, and pulled her hand mirror from her apron to catch the ambient flash of lightning from the high windows.

"A clever trick for a scullery maid."

The voice was like dragging a blade across a slate roof.

Anya spun around. Lord Alaric Vance stood at the top of the grand staircase, a velvet dressing gown wrapped around his ageless, predatory frame. His eyes were entirely black, devoid of whites or irises, pulsing with furious, dark magic.

"I wondered why my reserves were draining," Alaric sneered, gliding silently down the stairs. "I thought perhaps a rival had infiltrated my home. To find it is merely the girl who empties the chamber pots... it is almost insulting."

Anya didn't run. She tightened her grip on her small, cracked hand mirror. "You stole their lives, Alaric. Your time is up."

Alaric laughed a cold, hollow sound. "My time is eternal, little bird. And since you have broken so many of my prized possessions, it is only fitting that you replace them."

He raised his hands. A torrent of pitch-black energy, shaped like grasping, skeletal hands, erupted from his palms, shooting directly toward Anya. It was a binding spell, designed to rip her soul from her body and smash it into the silver backing of the mirror behind her.

Anya didn't dodge. She relied on the one law of her newly forged magic: Reflect.

She dropped to one knee, bringing her cracked hand mirror up. But she didn't just hold it like a shield. She angled it perfectly, precisely forty-five degrees, calculating the trajectory of Alaric s dark magic.

The black energy hit the small mirror.

Anya channeled every ounce of her willpower, shouting the reversal incantation at the top of her lungs. The small mirror glowed with blinding, blinding white light. It didn't absorb the magic; it caught it, inverted its polarity, and fired it directly back at the warlock.

Alaric s eyes widened in sheer disbelief as his own spell, magnified by Anya s reflective geometry, slammed back into his chest.

The force lifted him off his feet and threw him backward. He crashed violently into a massive, floor-to-ceiling pier glass at the base of the stairs.

But he didn't bounce off.

Because Anya had already painted the reversal wards on that mirror three nights ago, leaving it primed and waiting.

As Alaric hit the glass, the silver backing liquified. The dark magic he had cast, now turning upon its master, grabbed him and pulled. Alaric screamed, a horrific, gurgling sound, as his physical body rapidly dissolved into shadow, sucked violently into the depths of his own mirror.

With a deafening CRACK, the mirror solidified.

Anya stood up, her breath coming in ragged gasps. She walked slowly over to the pier glass.

Inside the glass, trapped behind an impenetrable layer of cold silver, was Lord Alaric Vance. He was pounding his fists soundlessly against the barrier, his face contorted in absolute, eternal terror. As Anya watched, the stolen youth began to rapidly strip away from him. His hair turned white, his skin withered into parchment, and his eyes sank deep into his skull. He was finally aging, trapped in a prison where he could never die, but could never truly live.

Anya picked up a heavy, brass candlestick from a nearby table.

She looked Alaric dead in the eyes, ensuring he saw exactly who had bested him. Then, she walked past him, returning to the massive Venetian mirror in the center of the hall.

She didn't use angles or moonlight this time. She simply swung the heavy brass candlestick with all her might.

The glass shattered into a million glittering pieces. A chorus of ethereal, joyous sighs filled the grand hall as dozens of trapped souls rushed upward, spiraling through the ceiling and out into the stormy night sky.

Anya spent the next hour walking through Highcross Manor. She visited every parlor, every bedchamber, and every corridor. She smashed every single mirror she found, leaving a trail of broken glass and freed spirits in her wake.

The only mirror she left perfectly intact was the pier glass at the bottom of the stairs.

When dawn broke, casting a pale, grey light over the estate, Anya walked out the front doors of Highcross Manor. She left her apron and her chamois cloth behind on the marble floor. She didn't know where she was going, and she didn't have a copper coin to her name. But as she walked down the long, gravel driveway, she didn't look back. She had spent entirely too much of her life looking at reflections, and she was finally ready to see the real world.
