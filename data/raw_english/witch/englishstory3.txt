Story : The Cinder-Girl of Cobble Street
London in was a city built on coal, iron, and a pervasive, suffocating yellow fog. It was a city of relentless industry, where human progress was measured in the rhythmic, deafening pounding of steam hammers and the miles of railway track laid. But beneath the soot and the smog, in the cracks between the cobblestones and the shadows of the massive factories, older things still lingered. Magic hadn't died in the industrial revolution; it had simply adapted to the times.

Elspeth was an ash-witch. Most traditional witches drew their power from living things ancient forests, rushing rivers, the howling wind. Elspeth drew hers from what remained after the fire had finished its feast. To her, ash wasn't just waste to be swept away; it was the distilled essence of potential, the memory of light and heat reduced to a fine, grey powder.

She lived in a tiny, drafty room above a baker s shop on Cobble Street. Her rent was paid not in copper coins, but in silent services. Every morning, long before the sun dared to try and pierce the London fog, Elspeth would slip down to the bakery ovens. She would press her bare, soot-stained hands against the warm bricks and draw out the residual heat, weaving it gently into the fresh dough to ensure it rose perfectly, even on the dampest, coldest winter mornings. She also ensured the fires never raged out of control, whispering calming incantations to the glowing coals.

By day, she was just another grubby street urchin, a face lost in a sea of chimney sweeps and flower girls. She made a meager living selling small charms to the factory workers a pinch of hearth-ash bound in a leather pouch to ward off machine accidents, or a piece of charred oak to keep the damp out of their weary bones. It was a small, quiet life, exactly how Elspeth liked it. The authorities of the city, particularly the stern men of the newly formed Constabulary of Arcane Regulation (CAR), took a very dim view of unregistered magic users running loose in the capital.

Her quiet life ended on a freezing Tuesday in December.

Elspeth was cutting through a narrow alleyway behind the massive Ironworks Foundry when she heard it a horrific screeching of metal tearing against metal, followed by the panicked shouts of dozens of men.

Without thinking, she ran towards the sound. The heavy foundry doors were thrown wide open, billowing out thick clouds of black smoke that tasted violently of burnt grease and ozone. Workers were pouring out, coughing violently and terrified.

"It's gone mad!" one man screamed, clutching a bleeding arm. "The main boiler! The pressure valve snapped, and the fire... the fire has a mind of its own!"

Elspeth pushed past him, slipping into the cavernous, sweltering belly of the factory. It was pure chaos. Sparks rained down from the high ceiling like a deadly meteor shower. In the center of the room, the massive main boiler, a multi-story monstrosity of riveted iron, was shuddering violently. The temperature gauge was pinned into the red, the glass face completely shattered.

But it wasn't just steam pressure threatening to tear the factory apart. Escaping from the ruptured furnace doors were serpentine tendrils of pure, blinding white flame. These weren't normal fires; they moved with vicious purpose, lashing out like whips, melting solid iron walkways and seeking out anything combustible. An elemental spirit of fire, likely drawn by the immense, continuous heat of the foundry, had somehow gotten trapped in the boiler and was now desperately, furiously trying to escape, taking the entire building down with it.

Three foremen were frantically trying to reach the emergency release valves, but the magical heat was too intense, driving them back with singed eyebrows and blistered skin. If that boiler blew, it would level the foundry and half the residential block with it.

Elspeth didn't hesitate. She threw off her heavy wool coat and sprinted towards the raging inferno.

"Hey! Girl! Get out of there!" a voice yelled over the deafening roar of the flames, but she ignored it.

She reached the base of the boiler. The heat was a physical wall, stealing the breath from her lungs and crisping the ends of her hair. The fire-spirit noticed her immediately. A massive tendril of orange and blue flame reared back and struck out at her like a striking cobra.

Elspeth didn't flinch. She plunged her hands into the deep pockets of her apron, bringing out two fistfuls of pure, white ash the remains of a consecrated yule log she had saved for a dire emergency.

She threw the ash into the air, creating a thick cloud of fine, grey dust between herself and the striking flame. As the magic fire hit the ash, it hissed and sputtered. The ash, carrying the memory of the fire that originally consumed it, absorbed the heat, turning into a shield of glowing embers that held the raging spirit back for just a second.

It was all the time she needed. Elspeth darted past the flame and pressed her bare hands directly against the blistering iron hull of the boiler.

The pain was immediate and excruciating, but she didn't pull away. She closed her eyes tight and pushed past the physical sensation, reaching out with her mind to find the source of the heat.

"I feel you," she whispered, her voice lost in the roar of the factory. "I know your anger. You are trapped, burning without purpose."

She visualized the ash in her mind cold, still, the final resting place of all fire. She began to draw the immense heat out of the boiler and into herself. Not the fire, but the raw energy of it. She acted as a human conduit, channeling the destructive power of the trapped spirit and grounding it, forcing it down through her feet and into the cold stone floor of the factory.

The fire-spirit fought back fiercely. It surged up the metal, trying to immolate her, but Elspeth was an ash-witch. She understood the fire better than it understood itself. She met its fury with the unyielding stillness of the extinguished coal.

"Sleep," she commanded, pouring every ounce of her will into the word. "The fuel is spent. The fire is done. Become the ash."

Slowly, agonizingly, the shuddering of the massive boiler began to lessen. The blinding white light leaking from the seams dimmed to a dull, angry red, then to a soft orange, and finally, to a sullen, smoldering grey. The serpentine tendrils of flame retreated, shrinking back into the belly of the furnace like whipped dogs.

Elspeth held on until she felt the distinct, heavy thud of the internal pressure dropping to a safe level. Only then did she pull her hands away. Her palms were blistered red, the skin angry and tight, but she had saved the factory.

She stumbled backward, entirely exhausted, the soot on her face streaked with sweat and tears of pain. She turned to leave, hoping to slip away into the smog before the foremen recovered their wits.

She was too late. Standing ten feet away, watching her with a mixture of awe and sharp suspicion, was a tall man in an immaculate grey suit. He wore a silver badge on his lapel an intricate gear surrounding a flame. The Constabulary of Arcane Regulation.

"That was incredibly reckless," the man said, his voice calm and cultured, contrasting sharply with the chaos around them. "And highly illegal. Unregistered elemental manipulation on a grand scale."

Elspeth tensed, looking for an exit in the smoke. "I stopped it from blowing. You should be thanking me."

The man stepped closer. He didn't look angry; he looked intrigued. "Oh, I am. The city is in your debt, Miss..."

"Elspeth," she muttered, wrapping her apron around her burned hands.

"Well, Elspeth. You have a choice." He pulled a small, leather-bound notebook from his pocket. "I can arrest you for violating the Arcane Protocols of . Or, you can come with me to the Ministry. We are in desperate need of people who truly understand the... temperament of the industrial fires. The city is growing too fast, and the old safety measures are failing. We need ash-witches."

Elspeth looked at the massive, quieted boiler, then at the man's gleaming silver badge. A life hiding in the soot, constantly looking over her shoulder, or a life controlling the very fires of progress.

She slowly unwrapped her hands, showing him the blistering burns. "Do they have burn salve at the Ministry?"

The man smiled slightly. "The best in the Empire."

"Lead the way, then," Elspeth said, stepping out of the shadows and into the faint, grey light of the industrial dawn.
