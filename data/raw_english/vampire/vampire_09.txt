The letter arrived on the morning of his eighty second birthday. No stamp. No postmark. Slipped under the door.

He opened it at the kitchen table with his morning tea.

It said happy birthday. It said I have been watching you live your life and it has been a pleasure to watch. It said you chose well and loved well and that is rarer than you know.

It was signed with a name he had not thought about in sixty years. A name from a summer when he was twenty two and met a woman on a train who seemed to know things she should not have known and who made him an offer he had ultimately declined.

He sat with the letter for a long time.

Then he wrote a reply. He left it under his front door.

It said thank you for watching. It said I have thought about that summer often. It said I made the right choice but I have never regretted meeting you.

In the morning his reply was gone.

That evening he found a single white flower on his doorstep. No note.

He put it in water. It lasted for three weeks without wilting. Then it was gone. And so was he.
