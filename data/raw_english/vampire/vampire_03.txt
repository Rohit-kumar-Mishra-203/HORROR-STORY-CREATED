The blood bank reported irregularities. Small amounts. Taken carefully. Whoever was doing it knew exactly what they were doing and how to avoid leaving traces.

The investigation led nowhere for six months.

Then a technician noticed something in the access logs. One ID number that appeared consistently. Late nights. Always the same quantities. Always the same blood types.

The ID belonged to an employee who had been listed as deceased for three years.

Security footage from the relevant nights showed a figure in the correct uniform moving through the facility with complete confidence. The figure's face was never visible. Not by accident. By design.

The investigation stalled.

Then the blood bank director received a letter. No return address. Neat handwriting. It said I apologize for the inconvenience. I have been trying to find a less disruptive solution for some time. Please tell your staff they are in no danger. I have never hurt anyone who did not ask me to.

The letter was signed with a name that matched a death certificate from nineteen sixty one.
