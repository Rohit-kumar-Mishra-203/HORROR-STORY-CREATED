She had not aged in forty years. Her neighbors noticed but said nothing. People in that town did not ask questions that might require them to rethink what they knew.

Her name was Eleanor and she had lived in the same house on Marsh Street since before anyone currently living could remember. She was pleasant. She kept her garden. She brought food to sick neighbors.

She was also never seen during daylight hours. Her curtains were always drawn. Her deliveries arrived before dawn or after dusk.

A new family moved in next door. Their teenage son did what teenagers do. He started paying attention.

He watched for a month. He documented. He started asking careful questions around town.

He found an old photograph in the library archives. Eighty years old. A town gathering. Standing at the edge of the crowd a woman who looked exactly like Eleanor. Same face. Same posture. Same expression of faint amusement at the world.

He brought the photograph to Eleanor's door one afternoon. She opened it before he could knock. She looked at the photograph. She looked at him.

She said come inside. We should talk.

His parents noticed he seemed different after that conversation. More secretive. Paler. And he never talked about Eleanor again.
