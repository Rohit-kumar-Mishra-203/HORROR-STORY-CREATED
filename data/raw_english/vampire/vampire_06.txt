The painting had been in the museum for a hundred years. Seventeenth century. Unknown subject. A young man in dark clothing standing before a window at night.

The painting's provenance was unusual. It had been donated anonymously. Accompanied by a letter requesting it be kept on permanent display. The letter was dated nineteen twenty three. The handwriting matched documents found later that appeared to be from the sixteen hundreds.

Art historians occasionally wrote papers about the painting. About the unusual quality of the subject's eyes. About the way they seemed to follow viewers around the room. About the faint smile.

One curator spent three years studying it. She wrote extensively about the way the painting seemed different depending on the light. The subject's expression seemed to change. Patient in the morning. Amused in the afternoon. Something else entirely at night.

She worked late one evening and fell asleep at her desk in the gallery.

She woke to find someone standing in front of the painting.

A man. His back to her. Same height as the painted figure. Same posture. Same dark clothing.

He turned when she moved. The face was the face in the painting. The faint smile was the faint smile.

He said I come to check on it sometimes. He said it is strange to see yourself growing old while you do not.
