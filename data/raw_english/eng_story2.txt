Title: The Photograph

Maya found the photograph at the bottom of an old box in her grandmother's attic. Black and white. A family portrait. Mother, father, three children standing in front of a house she did not recognize.

She almost put it back. But something made her look closer.

The family was looking at the camera. All of them. Except one of the children. A girl in a white dress standing at the far right. She was looking at something off to the side. Her face was half turned. Her expression was wrong. Not the stiff, serious expression of old photographs. Something else. Something that looked like fear.

Maya turned the photograph over. On the back in faded ink someone had written a date and five names. She recognized none of them except the last one.

The last name was her grandmother's name.

Maya went downstairs and found her grandmother in the kitchen.

Gran, she said, holding out the photograph. Who are these people?

Her grandmother looked at the photograph. Her face went white.

Where did you find this?

In the attic. In an old box.

Her grandmother sat down slowly. She was quiet for a long time. Then she said, that photograph was supposed to be destroyed.

Why?

Because of what happens to people who keep it.

Maya looked at the photograph again. At the girl in the white dress.

The girl was now looking directly at the camera. Directly at Maya. And she was smiling.

Maya was certain she had not been smiling before.

That night Maya tried to throw the photograph away. She put it in the bin outside. Went to bed.

In the morning the photograph was on her bedside table.

She burned it. Watched the flames take it. Watched it turn to ash.

The next morning the photograph was on her bedside table. Unburned. Undamaged.

And the girl in the white dress was no longer in the picture.

Maya searched every corner of the photograph. The girl was simply gone.

She looked up from the photograph.

In the mirror across the room, standing just behind Maya's reflection, was a girl in a white dress.

Smiling.
