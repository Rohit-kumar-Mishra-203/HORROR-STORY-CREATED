The dare was simple. Walk through the old cemetery at midnight and bring back a photograph of the oldest gravestone.

Jake had done stupider things for less. He climbed the iron fence at eleven fifty eight and walked into the dark.

His phone flashlight lit the path between the stones. Names and dates from another century. He found the oldest stone near the back wall. Eighteen twelve. He took his photograph.

That was when he heard it. A sound like breathing. But rhythmic and slow in a way that no living person breathes.

He turned. Between two gravestones something was standing. Too tall. Too thin. Its arms reached below its knees. Its face was white and smooth with no features except two points of darkness where eyes should be.

Jake ran. He made it over the fence and did not stop running until he was home with every light on.

He looked at the photograph later. The old gravestone was in it. Clear and sharp. But behind it standing in the darkness was the shape. And it was not looking at the stone. It was looking directly at the camera.
