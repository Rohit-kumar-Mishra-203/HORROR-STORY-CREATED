The medical student chose the cemetery as a shortcut. It saved ten minutes. He had done it a hundred times in daylight.

At night it was different.

He was halfway through when he noticed the candles. One on each grave he had passed. Burning steadily despite the wind. He had not seen them when he entered.

He kept walking. More candles appeared on the graves ahead of him as he approached. As if being lit just before he arrived.

He stopped. He looked back. Every grave behind him had a candle.

He looked forward. The graves ahead were dark.

He understood without being told. He was being guided. The choice was to follow the lit path or the dark one.

He followed the light.

He came out the other side of the cemetery in four minutes. Normally it took ten. He stood on the road and turned to look back.

Every candle went out simultaneously. The cemetery was dark.

He took the long way home after that. Always.
