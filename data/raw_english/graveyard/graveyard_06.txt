The night before his funeral his family heard him moving around the house. Doors opening. Footsteps on the stairs.

They told themselves it was grief playing tricks.

At the burial the next day his daughter noticed the gravediggers had stopped their work and were standing some distance away. Looking uncomfortable.

She went to look. The grave was already dug. But at the bottom of it someone had scratched words into the earth. She read them before the coffin was lowered.

They said I did not want to go yet.

Three nights after the burial his wife woke to find him sitting in his chair by the window. Not transparent. Not ghostly. Just sitting there looking out at the dark garden.

She said his name.

He turned and looked at her. His expression was one she recognized. The one he wore when he had something important to say but did not know how to begin.

He said I left something unfinished.

She said I know. She said I will finish it.

He nodded. And he was gone. And she sat with the weight of a promise she was not sure she could keep.
