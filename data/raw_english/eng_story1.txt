Title: The Last Knock

It was past midnight when Daniel heard the first knock.

He had been living alone in the old house on Mercer Street for three months. The previous tenants had left in a hurry. No forwarding address. No explanation. Just an empty house with all the furniture still inside, as if they had simply vanished between one breath and the next.

Daniel was a practical man. He did not believe in ghosts. He believed in loose pipes and settling foundations and the tricks that silence plays on a tired mind.

So when he heard the knock, he told himself it was the wind.

The second knock came louder. From the basement door.

Daniel stood in the kitchen holding his coffee mug. He stared at the basement door. It was the only door in the house he had never opened. It was locked when he moved in and the landlord had told him the key was lost. He had thought nothing of it at the time.

Now he thought of it.

Three knocks. Then silence.

Daniel set down his mug. He walked to the basement door. He pressed his ear against the wood. Cold. The wood was ice cold despite the warm night.

Nothing. Just silence.

He stepped back.
And then a voice came through the door. A whisper. A child's whisper.

Please let me out. I have been down here for so long.

Daniel's blood turned cold.

He grabbed his phone. Called the landlord. No answer. Called the police. They arrived in twenty minutes. Two officers. Flashlights. They broke open the basement door.

The basement was empty. Dusty. Cobwebs on every surface. No child. No person. Nothing.

But in the dust on the floor, there were footprints. Small ones. A child's footprints. Walking in circles. Hundreds of them. As if someone had been pacing down there for years.

And in the corner, scratched into the concrete wall, were the words carved over and over again in desperate jagged lines.

Let me out. Let me out. Let me out.

Daniel moved out the next morning.

But sometimes, in whatever house he lives in now, he hears three knocks from behind a closed door.

And he never opens it.
