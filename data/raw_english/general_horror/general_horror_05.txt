He had kept a journal for forty years. Every day without exception. A habit from his grandfather who said a life unexamined is a life half lived.

On a Tuesday in March he opened the journal to write his entry and found that the previous day's page was already written.

In his handwriting.

He had no memory of writing it.

He read it carefully. It described his Tuesday accurately. Everything he had done. In the right order. In his voice. His particular way of describing things.

He told himself he had written it and forgotten. He was tired. It happened.

The next morning the new day's entry was already written.

He sat and watched the journal for a full day. Nothing happened. He wrote his entry at night as normal.

The following morning there were two entries for that day. His. And one in his handwriting that described things he had not done. Places he had not gone. Conversations he had not had.

He read the other entry carefully.

It was a better day than his. More interesting. Warmer. More connected.

He started to wonder if somewhere there was another journal. With his handwriting. Describing his days. And whether whoever was writing in his journal was living something better or something worse.
