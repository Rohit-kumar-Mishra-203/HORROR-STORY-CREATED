The town was famous for one thing. Every ten years one person disappeared without explanation.

Not violently. Not suddenly. They simply stopped being present. There one day. Gone the next. No body. No trace. No lead.

It had been happening for as long as records existed. Every decade. One person.

A researcher came to study the phenomenon. She spent three months interviewing residents.

She found one consistent element across all the cases. The person who disappeared had always recently expressed a specific thought. Not to everyone. But to at least one person in the weeks before they vanished.

They had said that they felt like they understood something now. Something about the town. Something they had not understood before.

She was careful. She was methodical. She did not let herself speculate about what that understanding might be.

On her last day before leaving she sat in the town square and felt something settle into place in her mind. A connection between things she had documented. A pattern that explained everything.

She drove away from the town immediately. She did not stop until she was in the next city.

She never published her research.

She never explained why.

She never went back.
